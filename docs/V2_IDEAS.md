# Version 2: ideas to fold in before the specification is redone

Barry adds ideas here as they occur. Nothing in this file changes the specification
(`docs/Sketchpad-V2-Functional-Specification.pdf`, SPV2-FS-001 Rev A) until the two of us have discussed it.
Then the specification is revised in one pass. Newest last.

**2 Nov 2026:** I-1 to I-21 are folded into the specification, Revision B
(`docs/Sketchpad-V2-Functional-Specification-RevB.pdf`), and detailed in SPV2-DS-001
(`docs/Sketchpad-V2-Detailed-Specification.pdf`). Ideas added after this go into the next revision.

| No. | Date | Idea | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| I-1 | 1 Nov 2026 | Plugin engines | Discussed earlier (Sketchpad v2 Architecture, section 10): output engines (SVG, PDF, plotter, DXF, CNC) and input engines (what produces a drawing). Must be settled before CP-0, because it decides how the kernel, generators and renderers are found and loaded. Open question: does "plugin" also cover adding new makers, shapers, connectors and finders by dropping in a file, the same way generators are added? |
| I-2 | 1 Nov 2026 | Every V1 figure opens in V2 | A hard requirement: V2 cannot pass CP-4 until Barry's real figures open and look right. V1's format is fixed and documented in `docs/FIGURE_FORMAT_V1.md` (layer `lid`, `objectVersion`, `role`, `includeInOutput`, named handle discs, `scale`); the import rules are at the end of that file. Each generator needs a "from V1 settings" mapping, written with its feature list. |
| I-3 | 1 Nov 2026 | Parametrics needs its own discussion | The sampling loops far more than usual (Loops x 60 samples per loop: 4,500 points at the default 75, all drawn and searched); reduce it to the usual. Barry's intersection routine works for some curves and is useless for others: find out which and why. Possible addition: curve tracing and simplification (sample more where the curve bends and less where it is straight, and drop points that change nothing), which would also cut the work above. In V1 the infrastructure arm already thins the curve to 720 points (v1-finish item 4). |
| I-4 | 1 Nov 2026 | Simpler commands, built on sets of defaults | Prebuilt sets of components that can be inserted as a group with mostly default values. Isolate the most used kinds of setting (colour, line width) into one standard panel shared by every object, instead of each object declaring them. Standard groupings of settings shown as a tab set. Relates to the settings schema (spec FR-6.4) and the commands table (FR-6.12.2). |
| I-5 | 1 Nov 2026 | A template registry | Generic templates (circles, rectangles, saw tooths, and more) each built by its own controls, kept in a template registry of their own, separate from the object registry, so Barry can add new templates by adding a file. Not covered by the spec so far. V1's guide templates (v1-finish item 6: rectangle, circle, ruler, bounding box, fixed in code) are a first step to replace. |
| I-6 | 1 Nov 2026 | What the legacy adapter must supply | Survey of the 185 pattern and gallery scripts in `docs/V2_LEGACY_SURVEY.md`: 88 paint with a bare `ctx`; 80 use `scriptInfo` controls with a `redrawHandler`; 8 call `armInteractor` themselves; 3 have draggable points; 4 animate; 20 use blending, transparency or gradients and 29 fills or text (these stay playpen scripts with their own canvas); 85 distinct imported names from 29 modules, all listed, plus the `drawState` fields they touch. Voronoi needs `d3-delaunay` copied into `vendor/`. |
| I-7 | 1 Nov 2026 | Intersections (Barry's word for crossings) | Intersections are very changeable: they move, appear and vanish as objects change. Later, perhaps a formulaic way to define them (which curves, which occurrence, by rule) instead of finding them by search. Relates to I-3 (Barry's intersection routine) and to V1's magic points, where a curve's intersections with itself are not offered (v1-finish G-10). |
| I-8 | 1 Nov 2026 | A check that lists removed code | Gemini, during the earlier rebuild, deleted code unrelated to the change it was making. A script compares every exported function and name before and after a branch and prints what disappeared, so a removal is caught mechanically, not only by reading the diff. Run with the other checks (`lint:globals`, `check:imports`) before every review. Could be added to V1 too. |
| I-9 | 1 Nov 2026 | Name the versions by Barry's own history | The present app is Sketchpad 2 (v.1 had the registry, manifests, Patterns and Gallery; v.2 split it into subsystems, made categories prominent and added Home). The rebuild these documents call "V2" is really Sketchpad 3. Rename it in the documents before the name sticks. Saved files keep `createdBy: "sketchpad-v1"` (the format only gains fields); `FIGURE_FORMAT_V1.md` gets a note that it means today's app. |
| I-10 | 1 Nov 2026 | Exploration must be cheap | A major function, not a side effect. Model: `gallery/Scripts/Curve Stitch/manyRowsColumns.js` (M parabolas by N rows sharing points; add copies with a slider, drag a shared point and its neighbours follow). It will grow to lay out different predefined shapes in a pattern, perhaps with a layer where one shape rests on top, told apart by colour. In the kernel this is a generator that repeats a shape over a lattice whose points are shared point numbers. Judge every design choice by how few steps a "what if" takes. |
| I-11 | 1 Nov 2026 | Beyond the flat board | A shadow box with floating pieces as places for lines to link; string on real 3D objects; 3D-printed curves extruded in space; separate layers mounted at different depths. Cheap now, costly later: give every point a z (default 0) from the start, and let output engines (I-1) include per-layer cut or print files (SVG/DXF for laser-cut layers, a 3D format for printed curves). Layered mounting needs little more than V1 roles and per-layer print. |
| I-12 | 1 Nov 2026 | Commands at three levels, each in a fixed place | Barry's two aims: reduce clutter, and a place where something always is. Level is set by what a command acts on: **global** (the app: help, archive, settings, Notes), **tab** (the tab's collection: new, open, categories, import), **subtab** (the one open object, script or figure: save, sweep, print, export, undo). Each level has one fixed place on every tab: global in a strip at the right end of the tab bar, tab under the left button, subtab under the caption "v". One commands table (spec FR-6.12.2) with `scope` and a `when` test; menus are built from it, and a command shown in two places is one entry, never a copy. Commands that do the same kind of thing (save, export, help, reset) are one command that reads its context, not a separate one per tab; help opens at the current tab and subtab's page. Unavailable commands are greyed, not hidden. Layer commands stay on the layer (number chip or right-click), not a fourth menu. First step: survey today's commands, sort each by level, note where it lives now. |
| I-13 | 2 Nov 2026 | Error handling without clutter | One error banner (`window.onerror`, `unhandledrejection`) showing the message, file and line. One catch per choke point (each layer's draw, each tab's activation), so a failure is reported against the thing that failed while the rest stays live: never stop the whole app for one bad layer or slider position (the sketching purpose). Checks only at the edges: loading a file, registering a generator (settings schema checked once), reading a manifest. Asserts live in a separate checks file that runs after state writes when debugging is on, so the logic stays readable. A failed operation rolls back through snapshot undo. Not "make it professional": these named items. Built in V1 on `claude/v1-errors` (2 Nov): error bar, a daily error log in its own file (`errors_<date>.txt`), a start-up summary on Home, grouped Show Logs, registry and figure-file checks. Carry the same design forward: errors and other logs kept apart, a summary at start-up, one check per kind of file shared by start-up, open, save and a tool. |
| I-14 | 2 Nov 2026 | Nail colour cycle on the template | A printed nail can be coloured by its step along its arm, five colours repeating (blue to yellow ...), so a template needs no instructions: going up by a constant, it is hard to lose your place. Requires every nail to keep where it came from (layer, arm, step) through to printing; in V1 a printed nail is only a position. Barry has a colour laser. The idea may evolve. |
| I-15 | 2 Nov 2026 | Sweep, as Barry wants it | Form 1: a grid of up to 16 pictures over one or two settings (one across, one down). Form 2: more one- and two-setting runs shown as a very slow slideshow, 5 to 10 s a frame, with Pause, Back and Stop. Stopping, or clicking a picture, puts those settings into the open object (Draw) or the active layer (Figures). The v1-finish version (one setting, a grid) is a placeholder. Open: the order of frames over two settings. |
| I-16 | 2 Nov 2026 | Regression tests, run by one command | Automate what Barry does by hand (cycling through saved patterns). For every saved pattern, figure and gallery script, keep an approved copy of its output; a run compares each against it and reports what changed. Compare the geometry (the stitch ends and points each layer draws, already collected in V1 as `drawState.collect`), which is exact and is what nails depend on; also keep a small picture of each for the eye. The report is a page showing approved and new side by side for each change. An intended change is accepted with one command, which replaces the approved copy. Run before every review and at every checkpoint; nothing merges with an unexplained change. V1's `test:scripts` only checks that scripts do not throw. Decided (Barry, 2 Nov): added to the current app now (`npm run test:regression`, v1-finish item 14: 199 items, geometry where drawn through `drawLine`, pictures otherwise), and a required capability of the next version, built in from the start. In the next version it covers generators, saved figures and legacy scripts alike, and doubles as the import check for I-2. |
| I-17 | 2 Nov 2026 | More robustness | Seeded randomness: random generators and scripts take a seed saved with them, so they repeat (testable, and a liked random design can be reproduced; V1's Barnsley Fern and Voronoi cannot be regression-tested). A runaway guard: a budget of points and time, or a background worker, so a generator that loops too long is stopped with a message instead of freezing the tab. Crash recovery: session state journalled as you work, offered back after a crash or a closed tab. Kept versions: the last few saves of each figure beside it. An integrity check on open (welds to points, points to layers, layers to generators that exist), with a repair offered. Visible service status: when the Node service is down, say so and go read-only. Works offline: every library copied into the repo, none fetched from a CDN. Fast checks on commit: a git hook runs lint, imports and the removed-code check (I-8). |
| I-18 | 2 Nov 2026 | Every drawing goes through one drawing call | V1's drawing check (`claude/v1-errors`) found Linked Circles stroking circles itself, outside `drawLine`. In V2 the kernel's paths are the only way to the canvas, and the renderer is the only code that touches it, so the rule holds by construction instead of by checking; curves and circles are paths too. |
| I-19 | 2 Nov 2026 | Journals beside things, never inside them | One journal file per item (script, registry object, figure), in a journal folder beside them, one entry per line, added and never rewritten, readable in VS Code. Keyed by permanent names (registry key, figure name, layer `lid`); renames go through the app, which moves the journal. **Scripts are journalled at run time, not from git** (Barry works save, run, change, save, run, and commits rarely): each run whose source changed stores the source, the time, a small picture of what it drew, and any error, so each version of the code is paired with the picture it made. Diffs between runs are made on request. Barry can mark a run and add a note; marked runs are kept for good, unmarked ones are thinned after a period (keeping the first and last of each session). Any run's source can be restored or shown beside the current one. Git stays the occasional checkpoint; an entry may name the commit a run went into. **Notes** on registry objects and figures use the same journal, opened by one context-aware Notes command (I-12). **Learn:** on request, a pair of runs (code diff and both pictures), or a regression change, is sent to an AI for an explanation, returned as a draft that Barry edits and approves; nothing leaves the machine without his press, nothing is written to the journal unapproved. **Sweep studies** (I-15) are saved in the journal of what was swept: target (a script, object, or figure plus layer `lid`), a copy of the starting settings (not a pointer, so a replay shows what he saw), the one or two settings varied and their values, the form and frame timing, the seed (I-17), and the picture chosen. Two replays: faithful (from the saved settings) and "as it would look now" (against today's figure). Kept out of the figure file. V1 bridge (`claude/v1-errors`, 2 Nov): Script history reads VS Code's local history of a script (every save, not every run) to run or compare old versions; no pictures are kept and there are no notes. V2's run-time journal replaces it. |
| I-20 | 2 Nov 2026 | Ideabook hint scripts | Every ideabook image can have a hint script beside it (same name), a rough sketch of how it might be built from the kernel's parts, with its guesses stated, opened by a Run script button. Built in V1 on `claude/ideabook-scripts` (282 scripts, five folders). In V2: hints are documents like any other (generators and a few settings), so a hint can be opened as a figure and worked on; the image and the hint can be shown side by side; a folder's not-done list is kept; the routines the hints needed are the evidence for which generators the kernel must have (corner stitch, star of corners, polygon corner stitch, zigzag, growing chords, times table, pursuit, roulette, offset rings). Making hints for new images is a natural Learn task (I-19), always as a draft. |
| I-21 | 2 Nov 2026 | Stitch rules as a family, with StringThing's defaults | Barry: StringThing's philosophy was to declare little, default everything, and draw whatever was asked, however odd. `stitcher()` was meant to be the home of the ways of joining two arms (V1 knows only i to i; the chord rules are three near-duplicates). In V2 the connectors are one pluggable family: i to i, offset, reversed, every nth, part of an arm, growing skip, times table, all-to-all, mirror, and new ones added by file; every setting defaulted. V1's `stitchWith` and `growingChords` (ideabookRoutines.js) are the prototypes. |
| I-22 | 6 Nov 2026 | Cells, layouts and colour fields: one skeleton, three layers | Barry, after Alternating Shapes and Combinations (V1 gallery, 6 Nov): "this is really what Sketchpad is for: to help me conceive and perhaps move from the rigidity of most screen art." **(1) Cells.** Rows and columns of rectangles whose corners and edge midpoints are binding points, shared with neighbours; dragging one reshapes every cell that shares it. **(2) Content as a function mapped to an area.** Each cell's content is a call (a shape defined once in a unit square, or any generator) carried into the cell by a mapping built from its eight points: bilinear from the corners, edges that bow when a midpoint moves. The mapping moves the nails; strings stay straight, so a warped cell is the same nail pattern stretched, honest on the template. Folded cells (a point dragged through) are allowed and visible. **(3) Layouts.** Rectangles of different sizes fitted together: grid with merged cells, recursive subdivision (each split a handle), squarified tiling, packing (the gaps are content too). The algorithm proposes once (or on a button); then the binding points are Barry's to drag; it never re-fits on every change, or dragging becomes pointless. **(4) Colour fields** underneath, in the spirit of colour-field painting: a coarser partition of the same layout, edges sometimes on cell boundaries and sometimes across them; translucent soft-edged fills on screen, and on wood paint or stain applied before the nails, so the template prints the field outlines as a guide. One drag moves all three layers. Needs: generating and saving layouts (state captured and restored, akin to undo and the session layer). Prototype: V1 gallery (`gallery/Scripts/Curve Stitch/alternatingShapes.js`, `gallery/Scripts/Combinations/`). Much of the design can be done in Sketchpad itself; extensions come as needed. Related: combinations (two scripts as one drawing, controls kept apart only for convenience) may be how a cell is filled by any generator. |
