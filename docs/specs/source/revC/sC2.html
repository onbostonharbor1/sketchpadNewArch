15.0 ARCHITECTURAL REVIEW: OCEANIA COMPARED WITH SKETCHPAD [C]

15.1 Purpose and method

This section is for the owner's careful re-reading before construction goes further. For each area of the architecture it sets Sketchpad (V1, branch claude1) beside Oceania, says what changes, what is gained and what is lost, the implications that can be foreseen, and the alternatives. Where Oceania's choice has been proved by the prototype, that is said; where it has not, it is a judgement and may be wrong. An alternative marked viable could be chosen now at little cost; one marked costly later becomes expensive once construction builds on the current choice.

15.3 Drawing: geometry as values

Sketchpad. A registry object's draw() calls drawLine for each thread; drawLine both paints and is the only record of what was drawn (bounds, stitch ends for printing are collected as side effects). infrastructure() repeats what draw() knows so discs can be placed.

Oceania. A generator returns paths, stitches and named points as plain data; painting, hit-testing, printing and comparing all read that data.

Gained. Printing, nail checks, regression and attachments read the same geometry the screen shows, so they cannot disagree. Geometry can be computed off screen (sweeps, thumbnails) without borrowing the canvas.

Lost. The immediacy of "write a few ctx calls and see it". A playful idea now needs a generator, or stays a playpen script (which still paints freely, in its frame).

Implications. Every V1 object is rewritten (D-5). Effects that are not lines (fills, gradients, text, blending: 29 and 20 V1 scripts) have no place in a nail template and live only in playpen scripts; if the owner wants a figure layer that is a painted picture, the role image must hold a picture, not geometry.

Alternatives. (a) Keep painting objects and record what they paint (the recorder): viable as a bridge, but recorded geometry has no names, no infrastructure and no handles. (b) A hybrid: generators may also return a paint(ctx) for decoration that is never printed: viable, worth considering if the owner misses decoration in figures.

15.4 Objects: pure generators instead of shared singletons

Sketchpad. Fourteen singletons hold params and derived state; copies for figure layers are made with Object.create and structuredClone. The shared-settings bug fixed in V1 (Draw, Figures and Patterns sharing one set of settings) came from this.

Oceania. build(settings, kernel) keeps nothing between calls.

Implications. Objects that kept running state (V1's Pursuit, animated scripts, manyRowsColumns's seeding) must either recompute it from settings each time or keep it in the document as data. That is the right discipline for templates, but an animation needs a different home: time becomes a setting, or animation stays in playpen scripts. A generator that is expensive to recompute on every drag needs the memoisation of DS DR-6.6, which the prototype does not yet have.

Alternatives. (a) Allow a generator a private cache keyed by its settings: viable, invisible to the document. (b) Allow stateful generators for animation only: viable later.

15.5 Coordinates, transforms and welds

Sketchpad. Each layer draws in its own object space; its transform maps to the canvas; a weld keeps copies of a point in several layers' spaces in step, walking the welded group after each move (729 lines before the split, both Phase 8 bugs found there).

Oceania. Every point is in document coordinates; each layer converts them into its local coordinates to build. A weld is two handles naming one point (D-3). Moving or rotating a layer moves its own points; if one of them is shared or attached, the move is refused with its reason (owner, 3 Nov 2026: detect and flag; the right behaviour is deferred).

Gained. A weld cannot drift, because there is nothing to keep in step. Proved by the prototype.

Implications, foreseen.

  • Layer transforms matter much less. For a generator whose geometry comes wholly from its handles (Parabola), the transform has no effect: moving the layer moves its points. The transform still matters for geometry a generator makes from settings alone (a circle of given radius at a fixed centre setting) and for imported V1 layers. Whether to keep transforms at all is worth deciding now (15.12, item 1).
  • Welded groups cannot be moved as a unit yet. In V1 the owner could move a welded layer and its partners followed. In Oceania that is refused. The obvious next command is "move the whole welded group", which is easy to add (every point of every layer in the group moves together) and keeps the weld.
  • Rotating one layer of a welded group is the case the owner deferred: there is no answer that is both rigid and keeps the weld. Refusing is honest; it may signal a relationship that does not make sense.
  • A generator's custom drag (DS DR-5.2: a midpoint handle that moves both ends) must also obey the refusal when it would move a shared point indirectly. Not yet built; the rule must be applied there too.
  • Break apart (V1 item 7) produces layers sharing points by construction; it must create shared points, not copies, or the parts will not move together.

Alternatives. (a) Group move as a command: viable, recommended next. (b) Auto-unweld on a refused move, with undo: viable, but silent consequences are what the owner distrusts. (c) A constraint solver (points satisfy relations; the solver moves partners): costly later and hard to read; only if the owner's designs come to need many-way relations. (d) Keep V1's per-layer object space for handles, and solve welds as V1 did: rejected by both reviews; this is where V1's weld bugs lived.

15.6 Attachments

Sketchpad. Anchored discs at a fraction t along an infrastructure arm, or on a magic point by feature name.

Oceania. The same idea, made exact: layer, path name, and t by arc length, or a feature by permanent id; one-way (the attached point follows its path, never the reverse); cycles refused; a fallback position saved.

Implications. One-way is the right default for nails on curves. It does not express "this point is where two curves cross, and both may move it". If the owner wants a point that two layers both constrain, that is the constraint problem of 15.5(c).

Alternatives. Attach by step index instead of arc length (follows a nail number, not a distance): viable as an option per attachment; arc length is the better default because it survives a change of nail count.

15.7 Undo, state and recovery

Sketchpad. No undo. One state object held the figure, live instances, the saved snapshot, mode and selection, so choosing a layer needed a special rule not to mark the figure changed.

Oceania. Commands return new documents; history keeps snapshots with state ids; session state is separate; recovery checkpoints open documents.

Implications. Memory grows with figure size times undo depth (200 by default). For the owner's figures this is small; for a 5,000-point figure it may reach tens of megabytes, which the limits of DS DR-7.2 cap. Every change must be a command: code that changes a document directly is a bug, and the layer check cannot catch it.

Alternatives. (a) Inverse operations per command (smaller memory): costly later, and each command then has two halves that must agree. (b) Structural sharing of unchanged layers: viable as an optimisation, invisible to commands. Recommended only if memory is measured to matter.

15.8 Lists, files and the service

Sketchpad. Manifests per category, a Home manifest, directory registries, a start-up rebuild and a missing-file check; Vite serves the code and a Node service writes files.

Oceania. Folders are the truth (catalog worked out from them, small .meta.json beside items); one Node process serves the pages and writes the library; writes go through a temporary file and rename; version checks refuse an overwrite of a file changed elsewhere.

Implications. A file-system watcher on Windows can miss or duplicate events, especially for files edited in VS Code (which writes through temporary files). The catalog must tolerate that by re-reading a folder when in doubt. Without Vite there is no hot reload: after editing code, the owner reloads the page, which with no build step is immediate. V1's manifest fields (status, title edits) move into metadata files; titles edited in V1's Edit Manifest dialog must be carried across by the importer.

Alternatives. (a) Keep Vite for hot reload during development: viable at any time, nothing depends on it. (b) Keep manifests as caches, rebuilt from folders: viable, but reintroduces two copies of the truth.

15.9 Scripts

Sketchpad. Scripts run in the application's page and may reach anything: ctx, drawState, window, the DOM. Two failed after about a hundred earlier runs until V1 reset state between runs.

Oceania. Painting scripts in a sandboxed frame, geometry in Workers. Proved stoppable.

Implications. A script cannot change the application: no reaching into panels, no arming the interactor (8 V1 scripts call armInteractor; 3 have draggable points). The adapter must offer handles and controls as a service across the frame boundary, or those scripts become "preview only". The survey of the 185 scripts (reference R-12, V2_LEGACY_SURVEY.md) is the work list.

Alternatives. (a) Run trusted scripts (Barry's own) in the page as V1 does, sandbox only foreign ones: viable, simpler for his scripts, but brings back the state-leak class of bugs. (b) Convert the 11 interactive scripts to generators early: viable and probably the best value.

15.10 Controls, settings and the page

Sketchpad. params and controls declared separately, with drifted conventions (three keys for the widget name, two colour widgets); constants spread through the code; Bootstrap, offcanvas panels and 46 !important rules, the one area the owner hates.

Oceania. One schema for generator settings and application settings; one Settings command (section 16); a fixed page structure in the HTML and one plain stylesheet; no framework; no styles set from code except to show or hide a region (CLAUDE.md rule).

Implications. The page will look plainer than V1. Panels that V1 slid in from the side become fixed regions or a dialog. Every number that was a constant becomes a named setting, so the Settings page will grow; sections keep it navigable.

Alternatives. A small hand-written component for the few panels that need to slide: viable if wanted, kept inside the one stylesheet.

15.11 Units and output

Sketchpad. Everything in canvas pixels; for printing, a scale is set from one measurement.

Oceania. Documents in millimetres; pixels only on screen (a zoom setting); nails merge only when printing, at a merge distance that is a document setting.

Implications. V1 figures import as "legacy" units with their scale; until a scale is set they cannot print true size. Every V1 tolerance in pixels (snap radius, tip standout) must be restated: screen-feel tolerances (snap, hit radius) stay in screen pixels; geometric ones (merge, tracking) become millimetres. The settings catalogue marks which.

15.13 Overall assessment

Oceania keeps what Sketchpad does well (one file per idea, header comments, controls declared beside settings, a single drawing path, permanent names, the playpen) and replaces what the reviews and V1's own bugs showed to be fragile: geometry existing only while painting, shared mutable objects, copies kept in step, state of three kinds in one object, manifests copying the folders, and scripts sharing the application's page. The price is a rewrite of every object and a period of maintaining two programs. The largest risk is not technical: it is that construction runs long before the owner can feel whether exploration is cheaper (R-19). The stages are ordered so that he can try something real after each.
